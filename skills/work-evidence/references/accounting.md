# Time reconstruction rules

## Four kinds of input

| Kind | Meaning | How to present |
|---|---|---|
| recorded | Contemporaneous timer, attendance, or session data representing the person's activity | Recorded time; identify what the clock measures |
| recalled | The worker's retrospective account of actual activity | Recalled time, attributed to the worker |
| estimated | Inference from task scope, iterations, review and available activity anchors | Estimated time; note the basis |
| forecast | Work planned after the report cutoff | Forecast, never completed work |

An AI's historical estimate is an inference, not a measurement. Reconsider it when new information arrives. A user's statement that a category was omitted supports revisiting that category; it does not automatically establish a particular number of hours.

## Inclusive but nonduplicative

Count relevant thinking and research even without a finished artifact. Include product comparisons, drafting and revising prompts, reading outputs, supervising execution when actively engaged, manual testing, debugging, handoffs, messages, meeting preparation and presentation. Administrative work such as preparing the report may be a separate task if in scope; employer recognition remains an employer decision.

Do not add the full duration of unattended overnight agents to the worker's time. Do not add a meeting a second time when already included in a task. A person cannot simultaneously bill two hours for the same one-hour period by operating two agents. Split shared setup or cross-task communication once, noting the allocation. Team-created artifacts do not establish that the worker did all associated work.

Travel and holidays do not establish either work or absence. Use actual activity or the worker's account. A company may recognize a different amount than the personal effort estimate; keep these distinct. Do not apply an eight-hour day or compensation rate without an explicit agreed basis.

## Fixed totals and revisions

When asked for a fixed number, compute task-level best estimates and label the resulting total accordingly. Half-hours may be more honest than minute-level precision for retrospective estimates. If a worker proposes a total, reconcile it against tasks. Identify missing categories or invite a quick recollection; never invent a breakdown or rewrite estimates merely to make a claim look reasonable.

If the best-supported estimate is 61 hours and the proposed number is 80, keep the difference visible until there is a basis for revising task inputs. If additional recalled work changes task estimates, record the new basis. Do not imply an artifact independently validates the new time.

## End-of-month forecasting

Use the user's reporting month, not a presumed payroll cutoff. For a report prepared on the 28th, explicitly address the 29th, 30th and, when applicable, 31st. If three planned days are four hours each, add twelve forecast hours; never silently change past hours to preserve a previous target total. Replace forecasts with actual/recalled entries after the work occurs, rather than adding the actual time on top of the old forecast.

## Evidence and contribution

Meeting duration should use the individual's attendance, unioning overlapping joins or devices. Full meeting duration is only a fallback estimate when individual attendance is unavailable. A calendar reservation is planned time, not proof of attendance.

Documents, commits and messages establish chronology and scope. Distinguish asked-for, attempted, produced, reviewed, accepted and deployed states. A request for ten interviews or twenty posts is not evidence those interviews or posts happened. Preserve failed and incomplete work as effort where appropriate, with an accurate status.
